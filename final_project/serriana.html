Monstera Serriana

I like tropical house plants, specifically odd types of monstera plants. I can't just sit with a normal, greenhouse-matured Monstera deliciosa from Lowe's, though. Rare species of Monstera are more interesting to me, as well as other variegated fenestrating plants. These plants can take years to mature and reveal their final leaf form.

My Monsteras

My top four favorites out of the plants I have right now are M. Adansonii, M. Esqueleto (formerly M. Epipremnoides), M. Serriana, and M. Obliqua Peru. Two of these plants are nearing maturity, one is about halfway there, and one of them I accidentally killed. I hope to replace the one I lost with a cutting again soon, and start the process again. It's also important to stake your plants securely as they grow, as this signals the rest of the plant that it has a safe foothold for growth. This helps the plant mature.

About Monsteras

The genus Monstera contains 59 different species of flowering plants native to tropical regions of central and south America.


Most people are familiar with the common Monstera deliciosa, which is also called "Swiss cheese plant" and "split-leaf philodendron". However, the latter common names are applied loosely. The name "Swiss cheese plant" can also refer to the related species Monstera adansonii, while the name "split-leaf philodendron" is also used for the species Philodendron bipinnatifidum.

Related Plants
